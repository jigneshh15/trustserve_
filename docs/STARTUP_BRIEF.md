# TrustServe Startup Brief

## Problem and customers
Residents, tenants and housing societies need a convenient way to discover, compare, book and review local home-service professionals. Target initial market: Ahmedabad residential communities (assumption to validate through interviews).

## Value proposition
Convenient booking, service-category discovery, transparent demo estimates, professional profiles and verification workflow, booking status visibility and customer support. Verification in the prototype is simulated and must not be represented as real-world vetting.

## Revenue model hypotheses
1. Commission per fulfilled booking (pilot assumption). 2. Optional professional subscription/lead plan. 3. Society-level service coordination package. Validate pricing and unit economics with interviews before claiming traction.

## Lean Canvas
- Problem: fragmented discovery, uncertain trust, coordination friction.
- Customer segments: residents, societies, local service professionals.
- Unique value: organized marketplace with booking/status and verification workflow.
- Solution: catalogue, profiles, booking, reviews, support.
- Channels: society partnerships, referrals, local digital campaigns.
- Revenue: commission and optional subscriptions (hypotheses).
- Costs: hosting, support, marketing, verification operations, payment fees.
- Key metrics: booking conversion, completion rate, repeat bookings, complaint rate, verified-professional ratio.
- Unfair advantage: society/community partnerships if developed; not yet established.

## Business Model Canvas (summary)
Customer segments: residents/societies and providers. Value: reliable, organized service discovery/booking. Channels: web, society referrals, local campaigns. Relationships: support, reviews and notifications. Revenue: commissions/subscriptions. Resources: software, support operations, provider network. Activities: onboarding, booking operations, trust/safety, marketing. Partners: societies, providers, payment provider, cloud vendor. Costs: software/cloud, operations, acquisition, compliance.

## SWOT
Strengths: working prototype; booking workflow; role-based experiences. Weaknesses: simulated verification/payment, limited concurrency, no proven network. Opportunities: society partnerships, repeat service plans, local expansion. Threats: established marketplaces, provider quality, liability, trust and privacy risks.

## PESTEL
Political: startup/consumer rules may apply. Economic: household affordability and provider income. Social: trust, convenience, local referrals. Technological: mobile UX, cloud, identity and payment security. Environmental: route optimization and grouped bookings could reduce travel (future). Legal: privacy, consumer protection, contracts, taxation and platform responsibilities require professional review.

## MVP plan
Prototype (current): catalogue, accounts, booking/status, reviews, simulated payment, support and admin workflows. Pilot (future): interview users, recruit vetted providers, test one community, measure repeat usage and complaint resolution.

## Funding plan (hypothesis)
Bootstrap/student credits for prototype; pilot costs estimated and capped; pursue incubator/student entrepreneurship support only after validating demand and eligibility; no claims of grant approval.

## DPIIT readiness (not a legal determination)
Prepare incorporation/registration details, innovation/scalability description, founder information, proof of product and business model, and relevant declarations. Eligibility and current rules must be verified against official Startup India guidance before application.

## IP/compliance note
Use original branding/code or properly credited dependencies/assets. Obtain consent before collecting personal data. Publish privacy, terms, refund/cancellation, complaint and retention policies. Verify local legal/tax obligations and platform rules with a qualified adviser.

## Digital marketing experiment
Test society notice/QR, referral program, local search content and provider onboarding outreach; track attributable leads, booking conversion and cost per acquired customer.

## E-commerce/logistics
Discovery -> service/pro profile -> booking request -> confirmation/status updates -> service fulfilment -> demo payment record -> review/support. Fulfilment currently remains a prototype workflow; real operations and service guarantees are not established.
